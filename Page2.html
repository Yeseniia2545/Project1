Collection

The Museum holds over 416,000 items related to the wars and armed conflicts of the 20th and 21st centuries on Ukrainian territory and involving Ukrainians. The collection began to take shape long before the Museum's opening, in the 1940s, and was initially primarily associated with the combat operations and participants of the Second World War. It preserved weapons, uniforms, photographs, and personal belongings of servicemen from the regular and paramilitary formations of various countries on the Eastern European front. Gradually, its scope expanded through collections related to notable figures and artifacts representing the themes of local wars and armed conflicts of the 20th century. From the 1990s to the present day, the collection's growth and development have been significantly driven by a socio-humanitarian emphasis, focusing on the personal stories of people—both armed and unarmed—who found themselves at the epicenter of two world wars, experienced the effects of tyranny, became victims of genocides, and despite everything, offered their own physical or spiritual resistance to the aggressor. Furthermore, in extremely complex, often unfavorable circumstances, they embarked on a sacrificial path of struggle for Ukraine's national independence, state sovereignty, and territorial integrity in the 20th and 21st centuries. This is precisely why in our Museum, you can see an incredibly diverse range of exhibits that tell of different times, events, and functions.

For Example:

1.The Model 1882 officer's sword:

The Model 1882 officer's sword was a standardized pattern for officers in the French army. Its design is derived from the swords of the colonial troops. Production was established by the BALP manufactory in Saint-Étienne. Mass production of the 1882 model swords occurred during the First World War. They were used by engineers, colonial troops (except artillery), gendarmerie, chasseurs, mountain infantry (chasseurs alpins), and the Republican Guard. The sword was officially removed from service on August 13, 1925, but it is still in use by the cadets of the National School of Fire Officers.
